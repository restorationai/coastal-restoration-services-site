## Why Los Olivos Properties See Smoke Damage Issues

A lot of the housing stock here is rural: ranch homes, converted barns, and vineyard estate properties with large outbuildings, many built decades before current fire codes required sealed attic venting or modern HVAC filtration. Those gaps are exactly where smoke infiltrates during a nearby wildfire, even if the flames stay miles away. Properties with well and septic systems, common throughout unincorporated parts of the valley, also tend to have older electrical and HVAC components that are more vulnerable to interior fires starting in the first place. Combine that with hot, dry summers and offshore wind events that can push wildfire smoke through the valley for days, and smoke exposure here is rarely a one-time, contained event.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Los Olivos

We start with an assessment to separate what's salvageable from what isn't, because smoke type matters. Wet smoke from a slow-burning fire leaves a thick, sticky residue that smears if you wipe it wrong; dry smoke from a fast fire leaves powdery soot that's easier to vacuum but spreads further through HVAC ducts. Once we know what we're dealing with, we contain the affected area, run air scrubbers with HEPA filtration to pull particulate out of the air, and work through structural cleaning: wood surfaces, drywall, fixtures, and ductwork. For vineyard properties and larger rural homes with outbuildings, that often means treating detached structures separately since smoke doesn't always travel evenly across a property. Odor control is handled last, using thermal fogging or hydroxyl generators depending on how deep the smoke penetrated the materials, not just surface deodorizing, which only masks the problem temporarily.

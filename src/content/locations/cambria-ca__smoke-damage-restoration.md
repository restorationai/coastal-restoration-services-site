## Why Cambria Properties See Smoke Damage Issues

Cambria's housing stock leans older and wood-heavy: board-and-batten cottages, redwood siding, exposed beam ceilings, built when the town was still a sleepy ranching and lumber outpost rather than a tourist destination. Those materials are porous. Soot and smoke odor work into open-grain wood and older plaster in a way they don't in newer drywall construction, and the persistent coastal fog keeps humidity high enough that residue can re-activate and smell stronger on damp mornings weeks after a fire is out. Add in the number of homes here that still rely on wood stoves or fireplaces for primary heat through the foggy season, and small chimney or flue-related smoke events are more common than in warmer inland towns.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Cambria

We start with a walkthrough to separate dry smoke residue (from fast, hot-burning fires) from wet smoke residue (from smoldering or low-oxygen fires), because they respond to completely different cleaning agents and the wrong approach just smears soot deeper into wood grain. From there we seal off unaffected rooms, run HEPA air scrubbing to pull particulate out of the air before it resettles, and hand-clean or media-blast exposed wood surfaces rather than relying only on surface wipe-downs, since Cambria's open-beam and tongue-and-groove ceilings hide soot in grooves that flat cleaning misses. Odor work is its own phase: thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment to neutralize smoke molecules bonded into porous material, not just mask them. Our teams hold IICRC Certified Firm status, with WRT and ASD training that informs how we sequence drying alongside the cleaning so damp coastal air doesn't undo the work.

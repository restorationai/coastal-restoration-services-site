## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Templeton

We start by identifying the type of soot involved, because wet smoke from a slow-burning household fire behaves differently than the dry, fine particulate from a wildfire event, and each calls for different cleaning agents. Porous materials like drywall, insulation, and fabric upholstery often can't be fully deodorized and may need to be removed, while hard surfaces, cabinetry, and structural wood get cleaned with HEPA vacuuming, chemical sponges, and specialized deodorizing agents matched to the residue type. Because many Templeton properties run HVAC systems that also handle vineyard dust and agricultural particulate through the dry months, we inspect and often clean ductwork separately. Thermal fogging or ozone treatment follows once surfaces are clean, to break down odor molecules trapped in less accessible areas like wall cavities and subflooring.

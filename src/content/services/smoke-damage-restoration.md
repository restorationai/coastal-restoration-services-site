## What smoke damage restoration actually involves

Smoke damage restoration is not the same job as fire damage reconstruction. It's the cleanup, neutralization, and deodorization of residue left behind after a fire, whether that fire was in the structure itself or drifted in from a wildfire miles away. The work depends heavily on what kind of soot you're dealing with. Protein soot from a kitchen fire is a thin, nearly invisible film that smells intensely and is tough to see until it's already discolored cabinetry. Synthetic soot from burning plastics and furnishings is oily, smears easily, and spreads further through a home's air handling system. Wildfire ash, common here given Santa Maria's exposure to Central Coast wildfire seasons, tends to be drier and more alkaline, and it infiltrates through window gaps, attic vents, and return air ducts even when the fire itself never touched the property.

A real cleanup involves HEPA vacuuming of dry residue before any wet wiping (wiping first just grinds soot deeper into porous materials), ultra-low-volume fogging to carry deodorizing agents into the same air spaces the smoke penetrated, and in heavier cases, ozone or hydroxyl treatment to break down odor molecules rather than mask them. Hard surfaces get wet cleaning with appropriate agents matched to the soot type. Soft goods, upholstery, and HVAC components often need to be removed, cleaned off-site, or in some cases replaced.

## Our process

1. **Soot and residue characterization.** We identify whether you're dealing with protein, synthetic, or wildfire ash residue, since the cleaning agents and approach differ for each.
2. **Source and HVAC containment.** Ductwork and air handlers get isolated and inspected early, since a system that keeps circulating residue will undo cleaning work in other rooms.
3. **Dry and wet residue removal.** HEPA vacuuming first, then wet cleaning of walls, ceilings, hard flooring, and fixtures with residue-appropriate solutions.
4. **Odor neutralization.** Thermal fogging or ozone/hydroxyl treatment targets odor that has penetrated into drywall, subfloor, and framing, not just what's visible on the surface.
5. **Contents evaluation and pack-out.** Textiles, upholstery, and personal items that can be salvaged are cleaned ultrasonically or through specialized laundering; items beyond recovery are documented for your claim.

## What separates a good smoke damage response from a bad one

The most common shortcut is treating odor as a masking problem instead of a residue problem. Spraying a deodorizer over soot that's still embedded in a wall cavity will smell fine for a week and then come back once humidity shifts. A thorough response identifies where residue traveled, which almost always includes return air ducts, behind outlet covers, and inside cabinetry, not just the rooms where the fire or smoke was visible.

Another frequent miss is acid etching. Soot is acidic, and chrome fixtures, glass, and some metals can corrode within days if untreated. Adjusters increasingly look for documentation showing when cleaning began relative to the loss, because that window affects what's classified as secondary damage versus original fire damage. A documented scope with pre- and post-treatment readings, photos of affected materials, and a clear timeline protects your claim and gives the adjuster what they need without back-and-forth.

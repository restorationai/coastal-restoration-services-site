## What Trauma Scene Cleanup actually involves

Trauma scene cleaning is not the same as a deep clean. Blood and other bodily fluids penetrate porous materials, carpet, padding, drywall, and wood subfloor, within minutes, which means visible cleanup often leaves contamination behind. Our technicians arrive in full PPE and work the scene as a biohazard site: affected porous materials are typically removed rather than cleaned, hard surfaces are disinfected with EPA-registered, hospital-grade products, and the area is treated for odor at the source rather than masked. Depending on the scope, work can run from a few hours for a contained area to a couple of days when flooring or wall cavities are involved. We coordinate timing with family members, property managers, or estate representatives so no one has to be present during the work.

## Our process

1. **PPE staging and scene containment.** The area is sealed off and technicians suit up before any material is disturbed, limiting cross-contamination to the rest of the home.
2. **Removal of bio-contaminated materials.** Carpet, padding, and other porous materials that cannot be reliably decontaminated are bagged and removed under OSHA bloodborne pathogen handling practices.
3. **Enzymatic treatment and disinfection.** Remaining surfaces are treated with enzymatic cleaners and EPA-registered disinfectants to break down residual organic material, not just visible staining.
4. **Odor control and air quality.** HEPA filtration and odor-neutralizing treatment address the source material rather than covering the smell, since odor in these cases usually means contamination is still present somewhere.
5. **Waste manifest and disposal documentation.** Biohazard waste is logged, packaged, and transported for proper disposal, with documentation kept for insurance, property management, or estate records.

## What separates a good trauma cleanup response from a bad one

The most common failure in this work is treating it like a stain job: wiping surfaces clean without checking whether fluid has already reached the subfloor, baseboards, or wall cavity behind the drywall. A surface that looks clean under normal light can still test positive under UV or ATP testing, and odor that returns a week later almost always means the source material was never fully removed. Good operators document the scope with photos before and after each stage, keep a clear chain of custody for anything removed from the home, and disinfect per OSHA bloodborne pathogen guidance rather than general household standards. Insurance adjusters reviewing these claims generally want to see itemized documentation of what was removed, what was disinfected, and what testing confirmed the space was safe to re-occupy, which is why we build that documentation into every job from the start.

## What should you actually check before hiring a mold remediation company?

Check three things before you sign anything: an active California contractor license, IICRC mold-specific training (not just a general restoration badge), and a written scope that separates containment, removal, and clearance testing as distinct line items. A company that skips containment plastic or skips a post-remediation clearance check is cutting corners that let spores re-settle elsewhere in the house.

It also helps to understand what you're dealing with before a crew arrives. The [EPA's guidance on mold cleanup](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home) notes mold can begin growing on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours, which is why a fast response after any leak matters more than most homeowners expect. If you're not sure whether what you're looking at is actually mold or just surface discoloration, our guide on [how to test for mold](/blog/how-to-test-for-mold/) walks through the DIY kit versus professional inspection decision, and our [signs of hidden mold](/blog/signs-of-hidden-mold/) post covers the musty-smell-but-no-visible-growth cases that are common in older Santa Maria Valley homes with crawl spaces.

## What does the mold remediation process actually involve?

Professional mold remediation follows a set sequence: contain the affected area with poly sheeting and negative air pressure, remove and bag contaminated porous materials, HEPA-vacuum and treat remaining surfaces, then run a clearance test before the containment comes down. Skipping any one of these steps is how mold comes back within a season.

The source of the moisture has to be fixed in the same visit, or the remediation is temporary. That's the gap a lot of mold-only outfits leave: they clear the visible growth but never check why the drywall was wet in the first place. A firm with water damage restoration and structural drying credentials, like WRT and ASD, is built to close that gap in one job rather than requiring a second contractor.

Either way, that water isn't the same as a burst supply line. Sewage backup is classified as Category 3 water, meaning it's grossly contaminated and unsafe to handle without protective equipment. Who you call first, and how fast, affects both your family's health and whether your insurance claim holds up later.

## Who do I call first, a plumber or a restoration company?

Call a plumber first only if the backup is still actively caused by a blockage that just needs to be cleared, like a clogged main line that hasn't yet overflowed. If a fixture has already overflowed and water has touched flooring, walls, or belongings, call a restoration company at the same time, since contaminated water needs extraction and treatment, not just a cleared drain.

A plumber's job stops at the pipe: snaking the line, finding a collapsed section, or confirming the blockage is cleared. They're not equipped to extract contaminated water, pull out affected flooring, or apply the antimicrobial treatment Category 3 water requires under the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/). If sewage has already reached your floors, a restoration crew needs to be involved regardless of how quickly the plumbing issue itself gets fixed. That same drying and extraction science applies to any [water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/) job, just with more containment and PPE layered on top.

## Is it ever a 911 or utility call instead?

Call 911 or your utility provider if standing water is in contact with exposed wiring, if you smell gas, or if the backup is coming from a municipal sewer main rather than your own line. Those are hazard and jurisdiction issues a restoration company can't resolve on its own.

If multiple homes on your street are backing up around the same time, the problem is likely the city's line, not your plumbing, and Santa Maria's public works or wastewater division needs the call. If you can shut off breakers to flooded outlets from the panel without stepping into standing water, do that first.

## Does homeowners insurance cover a sewage backup?

Most standard homeowners policies exclude sewer and drain backups unless you've added a water backup, sometimes called a sewer or sump, endorsement. Without that endorsement, cleanup and repair costs typically fall to the homeowner.

This is one of the more commonly misunderstood gaps in a standard policy, according to the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance). If you're not sure whether your policy includes that endorsement, check your declarations page or call your agent before assuming either way. Coastal Restoration Services documents the loss thoroughly with photos, moisture readings, and a written scope regardless of coverage, since that paperwork is what your adjuster asks for either way. For a broader look at what a standard policy actually covers, see our guide on [whether homeowners insurance covers water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/).

## What should I do before help arrives?

Stop using water in the house, keep people and pets out of the contaminated area, and don't attempt to mop up or vacuum sewage yourself. Beyond that, there isn't much a homeowner can safely do until a crew in proper PPE is on site.

- Turn off the water supply to the affected fixture if you can reach the shutoff without stepping into contaminated water
- Keep kids and pets out of the room and close the door if possible
- Don't run the dishwasher, washing machine, or additional toilets until the blockage is cleared
- Skip the bleach and the shop-vac; Category 3 water needs professional extraction and antimicrobial treatment, not DIY cleanup
- Take a few photos from the doorway for your records before you shut the door

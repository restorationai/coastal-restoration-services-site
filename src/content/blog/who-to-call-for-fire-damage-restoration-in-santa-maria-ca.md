Once the fire department has cleared the scene and confirmed it's structurally safe to go back inside, call a restoration company before you call your insurance agent or start cleaning anything yourself. A restoration company can secure the property (boarding broken windows, tarping a damaged roof), start documenting the loss for your insurance claim, and begin the assessment that determines whether a room needs deep cleaning or a full gut.

## When do I call the fire department, the gas company, or 911 instead?

Call 911 or stay on the line with the fire department if there's any active fire, smoke, or a smell of gas, that call always comes first, before anything else on this list. If the gas was shut off during the fire, call the gas company (not a restoration crew) to have service safely restored once an inspector or technician has confirmed there's no leak.

If electrical wiring was exposed to heat or water, don't flip breakers back on yourself. A licensed electrician or your utility provider needs to confirm the panel is safe first. Restoration crews can coordinate around a de-energized system, but they're not the ones who re-energize it.

Once the property is physically safe, that's when the restoration call and the insurance call happen, usually in that order. Filing the claim after mitigation has started (not before) tends to go smoother because there's already documentation of the damage and the steps taken to stop it from getting worse. ## What happens when a restoration crew arrives after a fire?

A technician starts with a walkthrough to classify the damage by type and severity, following the [IICRC's S700 standard](https://iicrc.org/s700/) for fire and smoke restoration. That assessment determines which rooms need content pack-out, which surfaces can be cleaned in place, and which materials (charred drywall, melted plastics, heat-damaged insulation) need to come out.

From there, the crew typically:

1. Boards up broken windows or doors and tarps any roof damage to stop weather intrusion
2. Removes unsalvageable debris and separates contents for cleaning, storage, or disposal
3. Runs HEPA air scrubbers to pull soot particulate and odor out of the air
4. Cleans and seals surfaces to stop the smoke smell from re-releasing over time
5. Documents everything with photos and a written scope for your insurance adjuster

Smoke odor is its own problem separate from visible soot, and it often needs a dedicated pass with ozone or hydroxyl equipment rather than just surface cleaning. If odor is the main concern after a smaller kitchen or appliance fire, our [smoke damage restoration](/services/smoke-damage-restoration/) and [odor removal](/services/odor-removal/) pages cover what that looks like.

## What should I do before help arrives?

Get everyone and any pets out and stay out until the fire department clears the structure. Don't go back in to grab belongings, smoke inhalation and structural risk are the two things that hurt people after the fire itself is out, according to [FEMA's U.S. Fire Administration guidance on recovering after a home fire](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/after-a-fire/).

Once it's safe to approach the property from outside, take photos of visible damage for your insurance file before anything is moved or cleaned. Avoid turning on the HVAC system, it will circulate soot through ductwork that was otherwise untouched. And resist the urge to wipe down walls or scrub soot yourself; dry wiping can grind acidic residue deeper into paint and drywall.

If it's a cooler November evening and the house isn't safe to occupy, ask about temporary board-up before you leave the property unattended overnight. An open structure after dark invites weather damage and worse.

Fire damage in a Santa Maria home, whether it started with a space heater in an older Old Orcutt bungalow or an electrical short in a newer build, follows a predictable path once the flames are out: smoke keeps spreading, soot keeps etching, and every day of delay adds cost. The sooner a qualified crew assesses the scene, the more of the home and its contents can actually be saved.

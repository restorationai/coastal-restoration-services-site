## What should a storm damage restoration company in Santa Maria actually handle?

A real storm damage restoration company covers the full sequence from first call to final repair, not just one piece of it. That means emergency tarping and board-up, water extraction, structural drying, debris removal, and the paperwork your insurer needs, all coordinated by one team.

Splitting these jobs between a roofer, a separate water extraction outfit, and a general handyman almost always costs more time and creates gaps in documentation. When wind-driven rain gets behind a tarp that wasn't sealed correctly, or when drying stops a day too early, mold risk goes up. Ask any company you're considering whether they handle [storm damage restoration](/services/storm-damage-restoration/) and [emergency board-up and tarping](/services/emergency-board-up-tarping/) under one roof, or whether they're subcontracting part of the job.

## Is IICRC certification important for storm damage work?

Yes. IICRC certification means the technicians working in your home were trained against a published industry standard, not just given a truck and a mop. Look specifically for a Water Damage Restoration Technician (WRT) credential and, for larger or slower-drying losses, Applied Structural Drying (ASD).

The [IICRC's S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) is the reference document the industry uses for water extraction and drying decisions, how much equipment a space needs, how long drying should run, and when it's safe to call a space dry. A company that can name its certifications and point to the standard is giving you a real answer, not a sales pitch. Coastal Restoration is an IICRC Certified Firm and holds WRT and ASD credentials.

## Will they handle insurance billing and Xactimate estimates?

A storm restoration company worth hiring writes its estimate in Xactimate, the software most property insurers use to review and approve claims, so your adjuster isn't translating a handwritten invoice. That single detail speeds up approval and reduces back-and-forth on the claim.

Most homeowners policies cover sudden storm damage like a tree limb through a roof or wind-driven rain intrusion, but coverage details vary by policy and insurer, so check yours before assuming anything is automatic (see the [Insurance Information Institute's guide to disaster coverage](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance)). What a good contractor can do is document the loss the way your adjuster expects: dated photos, moisture readings, and a line-item scope. If you're weighing what a company first told your insurer against your own policy, our post on [storm damage cleanup costs in Santa Maria](/blog/storm-damage-cleanup-cost-santa-maria-ca/) breaks down typical pricing by job type.

## How fast should response be during an atmospheric river storm?

During an active storm, response speed matters more than almost anything else on this list, because every hour a roof stays open or a room stays wet raises the repair cost. A company that answers the phone 24/7, as Coastal Restoration does, can get a tarp up before the next band of rain arrives rather than the next business day.

Atmospheric river events along the Central Coast often bring several days of heavy rain back to back, so a single tarp or sandbag line sometimes has to hold through more than one round of weather. Check the [National Weather Service](https://www.weather.gov/) for active watches and warnings in your area, and ask any company you're calling whether their crews are actually dispatching during the storm or only scheduling for after it passes.

## What questions should you ask before you hire?

Before signing anything, confirm four things: an active California contractor license, real IICRC certification (not just a logo on the website), a written scope before work starts, and a direct answer on same-visit tarping versus a multi-day wait.

## What Certifications Should a Lompoc Water Damage Company Actually Have?

A qualified company's technicians should hold IICRC certification in Water Damage Restoration (WRT) and, for larger structural losses, Applied Structural Drying (ASD). These credentials mean the crew was trained to the [ANSI/IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s500/), the industry benchmark for how water categories, drying classes, and moisture documentation are supposed to work.

This matters more than it sounds. A Category 1 clean-water leak from a supply line dries differently than Category 2 gray water from a washing machine discharge, and the wrong approach can leave moisture trapped behind drywall for weeks, which is exactly how mold gets started. Coastal Restoration Services Inc is an IICRC Certified Firm with WRT and ASD training on staff, so drying decisions on a Lompoc job follow that same S500 framework rather than guesswork.

## Is the Company Actually Licensed to Do This Work in California?

Yes, the company should hold an active license you can verify through the [California Contractors State License Board](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/OnlineServices/CheckLicenseII/CheckLicense.aspx), not just a business card that says "licensed and insured." Restoration work that touches structural drying, demolition, or rebuild crosses into licensed contractor territory, and CSLB's lookup tool is free and takes under a minute.

## How Should a Restoration Company Handle Your Insurance Claim?

A company that's good at the insurance side documents the loss thoroughly from day one, including moisture readings, photos, and a written scope of work, and works directly with your adjuster rather than handing you a stack of paperwork to sort out yourself. Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, but typically exclude gradual leaks and long-term seepage, so the documentation has to support which category your loss falls into. The [California Department of Insurance](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/03-res/) publishes a plain-language guide to how residential claims are supposed to work if you want to read your policy's fine print before anyone starts tearing out drywall.

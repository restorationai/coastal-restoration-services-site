## Why does it matter whether the same company inspects and remediates the mold?

A company that both diagnoses your mold problem and sells you the fix has a built-in incentive to find more mold than is actually there. ## What does the mold remediation process actually look like once you hire someone?

A proper mold remediation job moves through containment, removal, drying, and a final clearance check, usually in that order over several days depending on the extent. Watch for a company that skips straight from "looks moldy" to demolition without setting up containment first.

The general sequence: the crew isolates the work area with poly sheeting and runs negative air pressure so spores don't migrate to the rest of the house. Affected porous materials (drywall, insulation, sometimes subfloor) get removed and bagged. Remaining structural surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated. Air movers and dehumidifiers bring moisture down to a safe range, because mold returns fast if the underlying moisture source isn't resolved. Only after that does a clearance test confirm spore counts are back to normal before containment comes down.

Santa Maria's coastal fog belt keeps crawl spaces and older Orcutt and Vandenberg Village homes damp longer than inland properties, which is exactly why the drying step can't be rushed just to close out a job faster.

## How do you verify insurance billing and licensing before anyone starts work?

Ask for the license number up front and check it yourself against the [California CSLB license lookup](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/OnlineServices/CheckLicenseII/CheckLicense.aspx) before signing anything. It takes thirty seconds and tells you whether the license is active, in good standing, and covers the right classification.

On the insurance side, most homeowners policies only cover mold that resulted from a covered water event, like a burst pipe, not mold from long-term humidity or a known leak you never fixed. A company that documents the moisture source, the date of loss, and the scope in writing gives your adjuster what they need to process the claim without a fight.

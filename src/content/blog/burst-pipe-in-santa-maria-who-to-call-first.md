Call a licensed plumber to stop the leak at its source, then call a water damage restoration company, such as Coastal Restoration Services Inc, within the same hour. Here's exactly what to do and who to call, in order.

## What should you do in the first 60 seconds?

Shut off the water at the main shutoff valve, usually near the street-side meter or where the supply line enters the house, and kill power to any outlet or fixture that's wet or near standing water. Those two actions stop the damage from getting worse while you figure out next steps.

If the water is anywhere near an electrical panel, outlet, or appliance, don't wade through it. Shut off power at the breaker box from a dry area instead, or wait for a professional if you can't reach the panel safely. Move anything you can lift off the floor, pull up area rugs, and prop up furniture legs on blocks or foil to limit contact with wet flooring.

Don't run a wet-dry vac or mop up a large volume of water yourself if the pipe is still flowing. Get the source stopped first.

## Who do you call first, a plumber or a restoration company?

Call both, back to back, within the same hour. A licensed plumber repairs the broken pipe or fitting, which is the only thing that actually stops the water; a restoration company handles everything the water left behind, including extraction, structural drying, and repairs to drywall, flooring, and cabinetry.

These are two different trades solving two different problems. A plumber's scope ends once the leak is sealed and the line is pressure-tested. They typically are not equipped with commercial extraction units, LGR dehumidifiers, or moisture-mapping tools to verify a wall cavity or subfloor is actually dry, and drying is time-sensitive in a way a repaired pipe is not.

## What happens in the first 24 hours after a burst pipe?

The first 24 hours determine whether this stays a cleanup job or becomes a mold remediation job. Standing water gets extracted immediately, moisture is mapped behind walls and under flooring with a meter, and air movers plus dehumidifiers go in to pull moisture out of the structure itself, not just the visible surface.

A technician will typically document the affected rooms with photos and moisture readings before any equipment goes in, since that record matters for your insurance claim later. Drying equipment usually runs for three to five days, with daily monitoring to confirm the drywall, studs, and subfloor have returned to a dry standard before anything gets closed back up or repainted. If the water sat for more than 24 to 48 hours before extraction started, expect a closer look for hidden moisture in cabinet toe-kicks, baseboards, and wall cavities, since that's where mold tends to start according to the [EPA's guidance on flood cleanup](https://www.epa.gov/flooded-homes). Our [full breakdown of the first 24 hours](/blog/what-to-do-first-24-hours-water-damage/) covers what a technician checks room by room.

## Does homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage from a burst pipe, including the cost to extract water and dry the structure, though coverage for the plumbing repair itself varies by policy. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) notes that sudden discharge from plumbing is typically covered, while damage from a slow leak that went unnoticed for weeks usually isn't.

Your deductible applies before coverage kicks in, and the adjuster will want documentation: photos of the damage, moisture readings, and a written scope of work. That's part of why calling a restoration company quickly matters beyond just the drying, since the documentation trail starts the moment extraction begins. If you're unsure whether to call your insurer or a restoration company first, our post on [calling insurance vs. a restoration company first](/blog/call-insurance-or-restoration-company-first/) walks through the order that protects your claim.

Once the water is shut off and the repair is scheduled, the drying clock is what matters most. Get extraction and drying started the same day, keep your receipts and photos, and let the restoration crew's documentation do the heavy lifting with your adjuster.
